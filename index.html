Summary

A historic milestone for Mexican polar science

CAMEX-1 is a milestone in Ukraine–Mexico bilateral collaboration. With the support of the Embassy of Ukraine in Mexico, the National Antarctic Scientific Center of Ukraine (NASC) and the Mexican Agency for Antarctic Studies (AMEA) signed a five-year collaboration agreement in August 2025.

Unlike any other country, Ukraine (NASC) invited a contingent of Mexican scientists and Mexican projects to develop science using Ukraine's entirely civilian Antarctic program scientific infrastructure, aboard the R/V Noosfera and at Vernadsky Station (Galíndez Islands, Antarctic Peninsula).

Following the evaluation of an open call for projects, specialists from five projects were deployed. A total of eight Mexican scientists carried out intensive Antarctic fieldwork in two deployments: in December 2025 and in February 2026.

The mission sought to obtain key data on Antarctic logistics, Geology, Paleosedimentology, methane cycles, and extremophile microbiology. Taken together, the projects were generally oriented toward addressing indicators of climate change and the biotechnological potential of Antarctic extremophiles.

Marking a historic milestone, this collaboration enables Mexican researchers to conduct original scientific research within their own institutions, leveraging an opportunity never before offered to Mexico by any other nation.
Strategic Framework

Background and strategic framework

Ukraine's unique and historic contribution to Mexican science, and especially to this partnership, provides Mexican researchers with access to world-class polar infrastructure — specifically the research vessel R/V Noosfera and the Vernadsky Academic Station on the Antarctic Peninsula.

The R/V Noosfera icebreaker gives all scientists access to Antarctica, with oceanographic research conducted aboard. Meanwhile, Vernadsky Station serves as the operational hub for geological, biological, and environmental sampling.

This infrastructure enables Mexican projects and institutions to overcome historical Antarctic logistical constraints and carry out multi-project campaigns within a formalized framework. It is also a cornerstone for polar sciences in Mexico and a commitment to environmental diplomacy.

Milestones

Key milestones and timeline

February 2020

AMEA takes the first steps in consolidating national polar science

The Mexican Agency for Antarctic Studies (AMEA) consolidated a comprehensive national polar science curriculum, leading to a formal application for Mexico's admission to the Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research (SCAR). This initiative was endorsed by the Mexican Academy of Sciences (AMC) as a member of the International Science Council (ISC).

March 21, 2021

AMEA's application was successful and Mexico has been part of SCAR since 2021

Mexico was officially admitted as an Associate Member of SCAR for the first time in its history. This membership grants Mexican scientists access to 45 international collaboration programs and extensive databases. Participation in Antarctic science is a mandatory requirement for any nation seeking a voice in the governance of the Antarctic continent.

July 16, 2025

An inter-institutional project led by AMEA secures a SECIHTI grant

Formal approval of SECIHTI project CIORGANISMOS 2025-91, titled "Strengthening the international link between Mexico and SCAR through AMEA, toward global scientific integration in Antarctica." This project provides the financial scaffolding needed to sustain SCAR membership for 6 years and integrate Mexican researchers into global networks.

August 2025

NASC delegation visits Mexico

At the invitation of AMEA and the Embassy of Ukraine in Mexico, a high-level delegation from the National Antarctic Scientific Center of Ukraine (NASC) visited Mexico to meet with Mexican researchers and sign a five-year strategic collaboration agreement between AMEA (Mexico) and NASC (Ukraine), in coordination with the Embassy of Ukraine in Mexico.

September 2025

Open call for projects in September 2025

A joint AMEA-NASC open call for scientific projects was issued, directed at Mexican researchers at Mexican institutions.

November 18, 2025

Project approval for the First Mexican Scientific Campaign (CAMEX-1)

Formal approval of the scientific projects comprising the CAMEX-1 campaign was finalized.

December 2025 – March 2026

Scientific fieldwork in Antarctica during the CAMEX-1 campaign

CAMEX-1 represents a historic milestone in Mexican polar science and diplomacy. Organized by the Mexican Agency for Antarctic Studies (AMEA) through a five-year strategic bilateral alliance with the National Antarctic Scientific Center of Ukraine (NASC), it achieved the first organized contingent of Mexican scientists on the white continent.

Expedition period: December 1, 2025 – March 1, 2026.
Strategic hubs: Operations based on the Ukrainian icebreaker R/V Noosfera and Vernadsky Academic Station on the Antarctic Peninsula. Mexican scientists departed and completed sample post-processing in Punta Arenas, Chile.

A multi-institutional team of eight Mexican scientists and doctoral students conducted fieldwork throughout the Wilhelm Archipelago, on the Antarctic Peninsula, to address global environmental challenges.

Biogeochemistry and Methane Cycles

Microbiology and Biotechnology

Exploration of extremophile ecology and study of novel metabolites (antibiotics/antivirals).

Educational outreach

Two real-time remote communications from Antarctica reached more than 250 students at ENCiT and CCH Sur of UNAM. A live class at UABC in Ensenada reached 60 students, in addition to further talks at upper secondary schools in Mexico reaching more than 50 students. Infographics and videos on social media further amplified the mission's visibility.

Diplomatic success

The mission serves as a "gateway" for Mexico's potential to participate in the Antarctic Treaty. CAMEX-1 has received attention and support from Dr. Claudia Sheinbaum, President of Mexico, SECIHTI, the Ministry of Economy (SE), the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (SRE) through the Institute of Mexicans Abroad (IME), and the Embassy of Mexico in Chile.

Institutional network

AMEA has signed collaboration agreements with CICESE, UABCS, UABC, and CIATEJ, and is advancing formalization with UAEMEX, UAEM, UNAM, INACH (Chile), and UMAG (Chile). AMEA has also facilitated collaboration between UNAM (Faculty of Sciences and ENCiT) and Taras Shevchenko University of Kyiv.

International Liaison

Key activities and strategic cooperation in Chile

Collaboration with Chile is particularly robust, involving the University of Magallanes (UMAG) and the Chilean Antarctic Institute (INACH). Meetings with UMAG Rector Dr. José Maripani focused on exchange programs and social service opportunities for students, while coordination with Dr. Gino Casassa of INACH centered on operational support for CAMEX-1. The "Mexico-Chile Academic Symposium" explored shared opportunities in Antarctic science, including bioinformatics, glaciology, marine current studies, aquatic biodiversity, and paleontology.

Diplomatic management

Inauguration ceremony (January 29, 2026): A formal ceremony was held aboard the R/V Noosfera in Punta Arenas to mark the operational launch of CAMEX-1.

Special guests included Ambassador Serhiy Pohoreltsev (Ukraine to Mexico), Ambassador Yuriy Dyudin (Ukraine to Chile), Ambassador Laura Beatriz Moreno Rodríguez (Mexico to Chile), Evgen Dykyi (NASC Director, connected remotely from Kyiv), Itzel Ortega (IME, Director of Liaison and Culture), María Pavlovzka (NASC Director of Biology), Natalia Shepel (NASC Director of Oceanography), and Captain Pavlo Panasyuk (Captain of the Noosfera).

High-level strategic meetings were held at the embassies of Mexico and Ukraine in Santiago to promote academic exchange, scientific cooperation, and long-term institutional ties. Consultations with Tatiana Clouthier (IME Director, SRE) were held to secure foreign ministry support for ongoing Antarctic science and diplomacy initiatives.

Additional meetings with Dr. Marcelo González and M.Sc. Alejandro Font of INACH identified shared interests in institutional liaison, academic exchange, and scientific cooperation.

University of Magallanes (UMAG): Meetings with Rector José Maripani and Dr. Máximo Frangopoulos focused on creating student exchange programs, social service and professional internship opportunities, graduate studies, etc.

Mexico-Chile Academic Symposium: AMEA and INACH organized and participated in the symposium "Collaboration Opportunities in Antarctic Science," facilitating direct knowledge transfer between Mexican and Chilean polar researchers.

Chronicle

CAMEX-1 February Deployment

Mexican science for climate change research

Logbook of the First Expedition of the Mexican Agency for Antarctic Studies (AMEA)

Days #1 to #5

I. Crossing the Drake: toward the end of the world

The expedition began aboard the iconic Ukrainian icebreaker Noosfera. The team, led by Dr. Pablo Lepe (AMEA), together with researcher Alfredo Yánez (UABC) and doctoral students Martín Villa (CIATEJ) and Izaid Ramos (UNAM), set sail with the objective of exploring the microbiological secrets of the marine sediments and micro-lakes of the Antarctic Peninsula.

The Drake Passage. During the crossing of the world's most turbulent waters, the team took advantage of the technical calm to run safety drills and immerse themselves in their hosts' culture by learning Ukrainian.

Sharing the engine room and common areas with delegations from the Czech Republic and Colombia reinforced the bonds of international scientific brotherhood. Colombia, which acceded to the Antarctic Treaty in 1989, has built a comprehensive Antarctic Science Agenda focused on ecosystem dynamics and climate regulation. During CAMEX-1, Mexican researchers interacted with the 12th Colombian Antarctic Expedition (2025–2026), sharing data and exploring the use of copepods as bioindicators of environmental health.

Preliminary meetings with NASC included joint sessions with Dr. Natalia Shepel (NASC Director of Oceanography), Dr. Mariia Pavlovska (NASC Director of Biology), and Captain Pavlo Panasiuk of the Noosfera to synchronize research protocols and explore future scientific cooperation.

Detailed technical tours of the R/V Noosfera provided the research team with an essential orientation to the vessel's advanced polar infrastructure after crossing the Drake Passage.

Messages were recorded for the Government of Mexico, the Embassy of Ukraine, the Ukrainian Antarctic Program, AMEA directors and volunteers, and the sponsors whose vision made this milestone possible.

Crossing the Drake Passage and life aboard the R/V Noosfera.

Days #5 to #20

II. The arrival: Vernadsky, a home in the ice

Days 5 and 6 — A historic anniversary. After days of navigation, the silhouette of Vernadsky Base emerged from the ice. The arrival coincided with the base's 30th anniversary, a gala event where Mexico had the honor of delivering a message of gratitude and brotherhood on behalf of AMEA. It was a joyful, camaraderie-filled gathering that marked the start of the stay.

Day 7 — Preparation and logistics. Before getting to work, the team toured the worksites and received safety and logistics training — essential for surviving and working in this extreme environment.

Vernadsky Station — the land operations center.

The real test began with the fieldwork. We weren't just looking for samples — we were seeking to understand life in its most resilient state.

Days 8 and 9 — Petermann Island. The team conducted the first successful sampling in collaboration with the Ukrainian biology team, collecting microbial mats, water, and sediments from three different lakes during 12-hour shifts.

Day 11 — The "Little Barceló" challenge. Perhaps the most physically demanding day: the team climbed a hill of more than 150 meters in a near-vertical ascent without climbing gear to reach a pristine lake at the summit. After collecting the samples, they returned to the laboratory to process them in an extended work session.

Day 13 — Island 8. They continued expanding the scientific scope, also supporting the Ukrainian team in their sampling tasks, consolidating a high-level scientific collaboration.

Day 17 — Irizar Island provided many micro-lakes of interest to both projects. While the Mexican team sampled microbial mats, the Ukrainian team sampled crustaceans, water, and sediments. Four sites were sampled on that island.

Fieldwork on Petermann Island, Little Barceló, Island 8, and Irizar Island.

Days #7 to #19 — Noosfera February Deployment

Aboard the research vessel

Days 5 and 6: On February 6, the team joined the celebration of the 30th anniversary of the base's transfer (formerly British) to Ukrainian hands. While AMEA's message of brotherhood and academic collaboration marked the start of part of the team's stay at Vernadsky, it also marked the beginning of the microbiological scientific campaign for the sole CAMEX member (Izaid) aboard the research vessel Noosfera.

Days 7 and 8: The journey toward King George Island began. Due to an unfortunate situation, a member of the Czech Republic expedition suffered an accident with a wrist injury, prompting an evacuation request through Fildes Bay in coordination with Chilean bases. Support was provided to facilitate communication between the Noosfera and the Chilean bases.

Day 9: Once the Czech expedition member was evacuated, the scientific program began with an additional sampling point. Near Liège Island and Base Primavera, the water column profile was taken with a CTD, along with water samples throughout the column, zooplankton samples using a Bongo Net, and a marine sediment sample at a depth of 1,061 m. Weather conditions during sampling were cloudy, wind at 17 knots, wind direction 60°.

Day 10: The next sampling point was reached south of Anvers Island and Palmer Station, above the Palmer Deep. The CTD and Bongo Net were successfully deployed. However, multicorer sampling was affected by weather conditions (strong snowstorms at 21–25 knots while snowing). That day was the first time the Colombian and Mexican expedition teams saw snow — it was truly special and emotional.

Day 11: Because the multicorer sampling attempt the previous day was unsuccessful, a Gravity Corer was deployed early in the morning in the same area. A sediment sample was taken at a depth of 1,340 m, which was split between the Ukrainian geology team and the Mexican microbiology team.

Days 12 and 13: Weather was not ideal for deploying the multicorer at points north of Adelaide Island. Nevertheless, collaboration with the Ukrainian scientists continued — they openly shared their research projects and encouraged learning.

Day 14: The team entered the Antarctic Circle, specifically Marguerite Bay, where despite poor weather, CTD and Bongo Net samples were taken at 3 points north of the bay. At the third point, south of Rothera Station near the peninsular coast, a multicorer sediment core and interfacial water sample were taken at a depth of 528 m.

Day 15: Worsening weather prevented multicorer sampling.

Day 16: An excursion was made to the British Rothera Station. During a visit to the station's construction site, the team met a Mexican engineer working for the company in charge of construction — a surprising and joyful encounter; patches and stickers were exchanged.

Days 17 and 18: Even amid poor weather, the last water and Bongo Net samples were taken in Marguerite Bay. An attempt to deploy the multicorer at the last point was unsuccessful. As an extra activity, a dredge was deployed to collect surface sediment organism samples for the Polish biologist aboard. The return journey to Vernadsky Base began.

Day 19: The last sampling day aboard the Noosfera. On this day, the CTD, Bongo Net, Dredge, Trawl, and Multicorer were successfully deployed over the Penola Strait; a sediment core and interfacial water sample were taken at a depth of 337 m. That night, Ukrainian and Mexican expedition members who would accompany the return to Punta Arenas, Chile, were welcomed back aboard. That same night, the journey home began.

III. Beyond the Polar Circle: the Noosfera expedition

The floating laboratory: Life aboard the Ukrainian icebreaker Noosfera became a positive and productive experience. Navigating the deep waters of Marguerite Bay and the Penola Strait, the Mexican team of Elsa, Laura, and Izaid seamlessly integrated into the vessel's operations to explore the Antarctic seafloor.

The deep descent: The technical heart of the mission involved deploying heavy machinery to reach the ocean floor. Using the Multicorer, the team collected undisturbed sediment surfaces and interfacial water samples directly above the sediment core; while the Gravity Corer allowed them to penetrate more deeply into earth's history. Each recovery was a moment of tension and triumph when the "mud" arrived on deck.

Mapping the column — CTD and water sampling: Beyond the sediments, the team monitored the pulse of the ocean. By deploying CTD sensors (Conductivity, Temperature, and Depth), they mapped the hidden currents and physical water properties near King George Island and the coastal zones of Rothera and Palmer stations.

The race against the cold — processing and preservation: Once the samples were aboard, the real work began. In the vessel's laboratory, the scientists worked in coordinated shifts to carefully sub-sample into sterile bags, weigh and quantify every gram of recovered material, and cryopreserve the samples to fix microbial and chemical signatures for future analysis in Mexico.

From the shadow of Rothera Base to the biological richness near Palmer, the Noosfera served as a mobile bridge between nations. Elsa, Laura, and Izaid did not merely collect samples — they navigated the complexities of open-sea logistics, demonstrating that Mexican science can thrive in the planet's most demanding maritime environments.

IV. Multiculturalism and diversity celebrated

Day 1: The 30th anniversary of Vernadsky Base was a multicultural celebration with representatives from Colombia, the Czech Republic, Mexico, Poland, and other nations. It was a very emotional day with speeches from the base commander, the NASC Deputy Director, and representatives of the different Antarctic campaigns.

Day 11: Aboard the Noosfera, a gathering was held to commemorate the International Day of Women and Girls in Science. During this get-together, emotional, funny, and courageous stories were shared about the paths that had led the female researchers aboard to science, as well as the role that women scientists played in the development of most of the male researchers on board.

Days 12–14: While carrying out support duties at the base, the team received a group of Mexican tourists (from Monterrey and Torreón). Their emotion at seeing the Mexican flag flying at the base was indescribable — the team became ambassadors of their country, sharing the mission.

Day 15 — Gastronomic diplomacy. The team had the honor of cooking for the 40 people at the base. They prepared chicken fajitas, rice, and handmade flour tortillas. The Mexican flavor won over Antarctic palates, crowning a day of hard laboratory work preparing samples to travel back to Mexico.

Day 20: Farewell and departure of the temporary engineering crew. Farewell speeches and contact details were exchanged between scientists to continue coordinating bilateral work remotely in the coming months.

Traditions and resilience: Not everything was work — the team also experienced the local culture by immersing themselves in the traditional Ukrainian sauna and, as tradition dictates, jumping into the icy waters surrounded by icebergs from a safe location.

This CAMEX-1 expedition brings back not only invaluable scientific samples, but the testament that Mexico has the capacity, talent, and heart to help global science better understand and protect this corner of the planet.

Multicultural celebrations, gastronomic diplomacy, and traditions at Vernadsky.

A historic achievement for Mexico in Antarctica: On December 12, 2025, the national flag representing an organized contingent of scientists was raised for the first time at Vernadsky Academic Base. Later, on February 20, the first sign from the Antarctic Peninsula toward Mexico was successfully installed — a donation from artisans from Querétaro.
Days #20 to #25

V. The return: surviving the Drake and the brotherhood of the sea

Days 20 to 22 — The fury of the Drake Passage: Leaving the protection of the islands behind, the Noosfera entered the open waters of the Drake Passage, where the Southern Ocean reminded everyone of its power. The vessel was met by a massive storm with 12-meter waves — true walls of water — that pounded the hull and kept the ship in a state of constant, violent motion for nearly three consecutive days.

Working under siege: Attempting to maintain scientific productivity was a battle in itself. With the ship shaking nonstop, even simple tasks like writing or analyzing data on a computer became a physical challenge. The team had to wait for fleeting brief moments of calm to make progress, demonstrating that in Antarctica, the ocean dictates the schedule.

Day 23 — Unexpected synergies: The return journey was not only about the elements; it was about the people. The Noosfera diverted its route to pick up a group of 8 Polish scientists and engineers who had spent three exhausting months building the new Polish station.

A scientific brotherhood: This encounter turned the vessel into a multicultural hub of polar expertise. Sharing stories of ice and sea, great synergies and lasting friendships were forged. These moments of camaraderie, born from shared adversity and a common passion for exploration, marked the beginning of the transition from Antarctic isolation back to the world.

The return crossing the Drake Passage — 12-meter waves and the brotherhood of the sea.
